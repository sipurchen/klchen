<channel|>The most idiomatic way to refactor this in Python is to use a **list comprehension**.

### Refactored Version
```python
x = [i * i for i in range(10)]
```

### Why this is better:
1.  **Readability:** It expresses the intent (creating a list based on a rule) in a single, declarative line rather than describing the step-by-step mechanics of initializing an empty list and appending to it.
2.  **Performance:** List comprehensions are generally faster than `for` loops with `.append()` because the construction happens at the C-level within the Python interpreter, avoiding the overhead of repeated function lookups for the `.append` method.
3.  **Pythonic Style:** It follows the principle of "one way to do it" for simple transformations, making your code more recognizable to other Python developers.
